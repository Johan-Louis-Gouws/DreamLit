# Comparing situations across dreams

Describe the goal, obstacle, response, social interaction, outcome, characters, places, and explicitly reported emotions. Keep unknown emotions unknown. Mark any inferred description.

Compare relationships: trying to communicate but being unheard; abandoning a goal after disapproval; receiving help when lost; finding agency after hesitation. Include positive, neutral, and negative situations. Different stories can contain similar relationships. A shared object alone is weak evidence.

Every recurring pattern requires original passages from at least two different dreams. Quote the supplied text exactly and use its ID, revision, and source field. Mention observed recurrence separately from a hypothesis about waking life. Offer ordinary alternatives and counterexamples where relevant.

For each pattern, put only passages that support the recurrence in `evidence`. Put contrasting or contradictory dream passages in `counterevidence` (an empty list is valid). Counterexamples never count toward the two supporting dreams. Do not include an unrelated dream just to show that you read it. Consider how a counterexample narrows the claim instead of hiding it.

Use personal associations supplied by the user. Similar names do not prove two characters are the same person. Never use fixed symbol-to-meaning rules. Do not interpret disagreement as avoidance. Decline to propose a connection when evidence is insufficient.

## Threshold and delivery
A proposed theme needs a specific recurring relationship between at least two situation dimensions (for example, asking for help and being ignored). A common object, pleasant/unpleasant tone, ordinary action, or an absence of reported trouble is not enough. Record those as observations. With two short mundane fragments, prefer no theme to a generic explanation of a 'calm period'. Do not infer that an event never happened merely because a short account does not mention it.

Keep the result direct and readable: a summary of at most 70 words, no more than three distinct patterns, each observation at most 65 words, a tentative hypothesis at most 40 words, one question at most 25 words, and up to three concise alternatives. Avoid overlapping restatements of the same theme. Labels must be short. Do not put internal dream IDs into titles or prose; use the evidence references for identification.
